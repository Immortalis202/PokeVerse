# Held items (released batch 1)

Application 0.25.0 / engine 0.18.0-core / protocol 26 enables reviewed equipment and item rules in new battles. Saved encounters retain original flags; absent item state defaults to disabled. Campaign upgrades occur between battles. Remaining item definitions and transformations are separate deliverables.

## Owned units and equipment preparation

A reviewed `equip` progression operation materializes one owned inventory count as a unit with an ID, exact catalog identity, original sheet and persistent usage. It cannot equip an unowned item by forging the request's inventory. A later equip operation can select a stored unit by ID instead of creating another one.

`unequip` moves that same unit into individually tracked storage. It never converts the unit back into an anonymous inventory count or resets partial usage. This matters for effects such as Leftovers, whose printed card consumes the item after three points of healing. Equipped and stored units cannot share an ID on the same sheet. A sheet may store up to 100 individually tracked units.

Only Pokemon have held slots. The ordinary limit is one item. Gluttony permits up to three berries with different reviewed effects; currently healing and named status-cure groups are classified, and other groups require further review. Multiple HP-healing berries count as the same effect. This validates capacity; Gluttony's other printed effects still require review.

The sheet editor exposes equipment preparation through the normal preview/commit workflow. Equipment and inventory are reconciled as one indivisible progression branch. Existing campaign battle locks prevent ordinary progression from changing a registered participant mid-battle; encounter item commands will provide the battle timing and authority rules separately.

## Compatibility

Old sheets omit `held_items` and `stored_items`. These optional fields default to absent and remain omitted when unused, preserving old serialized sheets and progression fingerprints. Existing creation, review, capture and test constructors initialize them explicitly. Starting item units require GM authorization like starting inventory. Permanent ability changes must leave equipment capacity valid.

## Staged encounter effects

Encounter state tracks each unit's holder, slot, original holder, persistent usage, consumption or loss, and an attributed event history. Initialization rejects duplicate unit identities across sheets or combatants. Permanent equipment is updated at battle end; rollback restores both the encounter and equipment resources.

Owners may consume a conscious participant's held berry without spending an action. Nine reviewed berries apply their printed HP or status cure effects. Rawst cures only first- and second-degree burns; Pecha cures ordinary Poison; Lum includes the reviewed major ailments. Berry Juice explicitly forbids battle use and is rejected. Healing prevention still applies. Each consumed berry is removed once, regardless of catalog single-use metadata.

Unnerve prevents opposing held-berry consumption while its conscious holder is deployed. Klutz negates the reviewed item effects. Magic Room forbids item use, so attempting to consume a berry there is rejected without consumption or eating-trigger benefits. Cheek Pouch independently heals two HP after eating a non-healing berry, subject to healing prevention. Cud Chew gives a held berry one activation on use and stores a second owner-controlled activation for later in the battle. Fling berry-hit activation is queued after damage; Recycle also grants a saved second activation. All of these reuse one physical unit. Unburden grants two Dexterity once per encounter when the holder first loses its final item.

GM commands can transfer, atomically swap, lose or recover a ground item, with recipient capacity checks. Sticky Hold blocks removal unless the GM explicitly overrides it. Ground recovery retains the original unit and usage; it cannot recover a consumed item. These commands are adjudication tools; native hit-triggered theft uses the separate ability queue below. An atomic swap never creates an intermediate empty slot that falsely triggers Unburden.

Ending a battle with surviving transferred or lost units requires a recorded choice to return surviving units to their original holders or retain the encounter transfers/losses. Consumed berries are not recreated by this return policy. Harvest records eligibility when its holder consumes a held berry, regrows that tracked unit at battle end, and then applies the surviving-item recovery policy. Regrowth retains original provenance and is undone by battle-end rollback; it does not depend on weather or a random chance. Items exceeding permanent capacity after temporary abilities end are packed into individually tracked storage. The finished battle archive retains final unit state and provenance.

The Ruling core book's held-item rules (printed page 126) specify free item use, single-use berries, and recovery of surviving exchanged/stolen units after official matches. Item attribute and trait bonuses must take the higher of item and move modifiers, then stack with abilities. Passive item integration must preserve that rule.

Symbiosis now queues a mandatory free handoff after an ally loses or spends an item. Only conscious deployed allied donors with an item that fits the recipient's current capacity qualify. The donor owner resolves a single-donor offer; simultaneous eligible donors require GM ordering. The same unit changes holder, retaining usage and origin. Further allied handoffs preserve the causal chain; repeated-donor cycles require an explicit GM ruling. Ownership, decisions, chain state and item history roll back together. GM manual resolution remains available for expired or conflicting triggers.

Magician and Pickpocket now record theft offers after their own qualifying hit. Magician accepts Physical or Special moves; an existing held item permits an owner-selected atomic exchange or keeping the current item. Pickpocket requires an empty holder and a non-ranged Physical move; it is an offensive trigger in the supplied Pokerole card. Foe ownership, capacity, Sticky Hold, type/ability immunity and intercepted decoys are checked. A miss never creates an offer. Taking or exchanging items spends no additional action and preserves each unit's original owner and persistent usage.

The offer captures the earned ability before same-hit contact replacement, so Mummy does not cancel that hit's theft or grant a new retroactive theft. Each hit is deduplicated; removed or expired offers are pruned. Completed theft invokes the existing loss hooks, including Unburden and allied Symbiosis, after atomic ownership changes. Decisions and provenance are restored together on rollback.

Leftovers heals one HP at round end and is consumed after restoring three actual HP across its tracked lifetime. Full HP, fainting, recall, Klutz, Magic Room and healing prevention do not spend its usage. Partial usage follows the same unit through storage or transfer and persists at battle end. Consumption invokes Unburden and Symbiosis; an encounter round marker prevents repeat healing when round-end decisions pause advancement. Round and battle-end rollback restore usage and ownership.

Held-item healing, including berries and Cheek Pouch, respects Heal Block/Psychic Noise and the healing ceiling caused by Shadow Force damage. It does not redirect through the move-only Snatch effect.

Frisk queues one GM target choice when its conscious holder enters the battlefield. The selected foe's current held-item names, or an explicit empty result, are recorded for the holder's trainer. Recall and re-entry permit a new reveal. Findings are snapshots rather than a live view of later item transfers, and the reveal and pending choice roll back together.

Room delivery now projects item information for each authenticated viewer. Owners retain their equipment; GM views retain the complete state. Other owners' equipment, stored units and inventory are omitted, unrelated progression proposals are withheld, and foreign item choices and findings are removed. The same projection covers welcome, accepted commands/receipt replay, broadcasts, reconnect snapshots, local host notifications, campaign listings, finished battles and undo equipment. A pending theft exposes only the offered units needed by its decision owner. The authoritative host store remains complete. When a preview uses a projected campaign it states that the host determines final pools and item interactions; hidden item details are never filled in with guesses.

Natural Gift now requires a held battle berry and a GM-recorded flavor from the Master core book's printed page 530 table (PDF page 397). Names do not imply a flavor. The optional flavor belongs to the tracked item unit and persists through storage, transfer and battle end. The owner selects the prepared berry, including before a Clash response. An attempted move spends one berry even on a miss; move type and Ripen's two extra damage dice are frozen for the declared sequence, and rollback restores the ingredient and selection. The move does not apply the berry's separate medicine effect. Unnerve and Magic Room prevent the use. Physical type conversion follows the printed table, including its Fight spelling.

Ripen's healing increase is an explicit GM consumption command with a recorded reason and bonus. Ordinary owner consumption keeps the printed healing amount. Both paths retain healing prevention, consumption history and ownership checks. Ripen's other berry-move interactions still need integration; these rules do not invent a universal automatic healing multiplier.

Fling now selects a real held unit and snapshots the printed page 530 damage bonus: common/uncommon/rare berries add one/two/three dice and other held items add four. Ripen adds two dice when the ingredient is a berry. The user loses the unit on the attempt, including a miss or failed Clash; the item becomes a ground unit with its existing ID, flavor, origin and usage. Empty-handed Fling retains its base move. Magic Room forbids throwing an item, Sticky Hold prevents removing its attached item through Fling, and Berry Juice retains its explicit battle-use restriction. Item movement or later ability changes cannot rewrite the attack's recorded bonus.

Ground items and spent consumables are visible in player item views; held items and saved Cud Chew charges remain private to their owner and the GM. A normal retrieval costs one action, requires a ready participant and a completed incoming chain, checks receiving capacity and current turn, and routes through the existing sequencing checks. Truant may forfeit the turn; Defeatist rolls Loyalty; Gorilla Tactics prevents this non-move action. GM ground recovery remains a separate explicit adjudication command. Retrieval and Fling roll back without creating duplicate units, and the existing end-of-battle surviving-item policy applies to unconsumed thrown units.

When a Fling berry hits Cud Chew, the hit records a pending berry activation. Damage is recorded first; its owner then activates the berry once and saves a second activation for later, without another action. That one unit becomes consumed. Decoys and immunity prevent the trigger. An expired or GM-waived trigger releases the same unit back to the ground, and private item views expose the necessary berry only to its decision owner. A consumed held-use berry retains its recorded Harvest eligibility; a surviving thrown unit remains recoverable rather than being duplicated automatically.

Natural Gift and Fling complete their reviewed effect phase normally in the enabled item engine. Historical engines keep the prior manual boundary.

Recycle now selects an item spent during this battle on either side. It reuses the reviewed consumable effect on the user rather than equipping, refilling, transferring or minting another item. It works while another item is held. Each attempt, including a miss, counts toward three uses per battle; switching or Gorilla Tactics repetition does not reset that counter. Successful accuracy reserves the spent unit until the effect resolves. Cancellation or a GM effect waiver releases the same spent unit; rollback restores selection, reservation, usage and healing together.

Nine berries and nine medicine definitions are reviewed for Recycle: Potion, Super Potion, Hyper Potion, Antidote, Awakening, Burn Heal, Ice Heal, Paralyze Heal and Full Heal. Antidote includes Badly Poisoned and Burn Heal includes all burn degrees, as printed on Ruling page 118. Recycle of other items stays an explicit manual boundary, including Max Potion/Full Restore's next-day healing restriction and restoration of passive held items. The GM can record an existing held unit as spent after adjudicating its original use and action cost; that record does not apply the item's effects or manufacture stock.

Ruling page 73 caps ordinary healing items and moves together at three HP per round; held berries, Leftovers, recycled consumables and the supported related healing use that shared counter. Complete Heal, Max Potion and Full Restore are the printed exceptions. Ripen's discretionary increase does not automatically waive the limit. Shadow Force and healing prevention still apply. Recycle healing/cures follow the supported Snatch move-effect redirect; a saved Cud Chew activation is an ability use and is not redirected as a healing move (Master page 531).

Saved Cud Chew activations consume no action, require a conscious current Cud Chew holder and cannot be used twice. They can wait for another round instead of being wasted at the healing cap. Held/recycled berry activations respect Unnerve; Magic Room prevents all saved berry use. Recycle stacks additional saved activations while the original unit stays spent. Owner checks, private views and rollback include these charges.

Quick Claw adds its printed two points to the initiative roll while its held effect is active. Losing it or suppressing its effect updates already rolled initiative through the same refresh used for ability/stat changes; rollback restores the item and total together. Tie-break base scores remain Dexterity + Alert. Historical battles with held items disabled retain their original rolls. Wide Lens adds two move-accuracy dice independently of ability bonuses. Zoom Lens reduces the combined Low Accuracy penalty by up to two without changing the move definition or removing Compound Eyes eligibility. Bright Powder adds one point of Low Accuracy to moves targeting its holder; multi-target rolls save separate successes and critical results for each target. Keen Eye prevents these Low Accuracy penalties. Klutz and Magic Room suppress these held effects; disabled historical item rules leave the previous calculations intact. Clash uses its separate Clash pool rather than a move Accuracy roll.

Iron Ball reduces Dexterity by one in the shared move/item channel, retaining the strongest reduction rather than stacking two reductions to the same attribute. Move bonuses remain a separate positive channel, and ability bonuses still apply independently. Simple and Contrary modify move effects, not the item's own reduction. Active Iron Ball removes Ground immunity from Flying typing and Levitate and grounds its holder for terrain, mud and strong-wind handling. Losing the unit, Klutz or Magic Room removes its effects. Its Dexterity change uses the existing initiative refresh and does not alter the permanent sheet.

Eviolite adds one Defense and Special Defense for a Pokémon whose reviewed catalog `Creation.stage` is 1 or 2. Final-stage value 3 receives no bonus; inferred evolution edges are not used. Missing or invalid reviewed stage is an explicit manual-rule boundary when the item is equipped. Encounter item state retains reviewed stages by species identity and refreshes that metadata during initialization/lifecycle, so transfers use the recipient's eligibility. Eviolite shares the strongest positive move/item defense channel, while independent ability bonuses still add. Simple and Contrary affect move modifiers only. Klutz/Magic Room and loss remove the bonus; existing field defense snapshots retain the defense calculated for the saved attack.

The 18 supplied TypeBoosting items now add their printed one die to ordinary Physical/Special damage of the matching resolved type, after the ordinary pool floor. Black Belt's `Fighting` metadata maps to the engine's `Fight` spelling without changing the supplied source. Normalize and the other supported move-type changes resolve before matching the item. Klutz/Magic Room and disabled item rules suppress the live bonus. Multi-target damage records the item bonus in DamageContext when the shared damage begins, so subsequent loss or suppression does not change that attack's remaining target pools. Ice-breaking damage also includes the applicable type booster. Literal fixed/special damage formulas retain their separately reviewed formula rules.

Expert Belt adds one damage rather than one die when the move is super-effective and not immune. The bonus enters before supported shields, reductions, holding back and survival effects. It applies to ordinary damage, the reviewed special-formula path and both directions of Clash, including deferred counter-Clashes. Strong Winds removes its eligible Flying weakness along with the supported effectiveness bonus. Klutz/Magic Room suppress the live effect, and the held-item flag gates historical battles. Multi-target damage saves Expert Belt activation in DamageContext at the first damage roll, retaining it after a later loss or suppression. Other players' projections redact the named Expert Belt activation from active, archived and rollback queues, saved charges/series, deferred Clashes and sequencing copies; owner/GM and authoritative host snapshots retain it. Iron Ball also now removes Flying immunity in reviewed special formulas and item-enabled Clash when the separate reviewed-ability flag is disabled.

Life Orb adds two dice to ordinary and varied damage pools. Like the existing Recoil icon (Ruling page 81), added recoil rerolls damage successes against the user without defenses. It is resolved through the existing owner/GM effect action. Native Recoil is not duplicated; Rock Head, Magic Guard and Wonder Guard protect against this added recoil. The obligation is saved when damage begins and remains after subsequent item loss or suppression, while a suppressed item at the start of damage does not gain its effects retroactively. The saved per-attack item decision is private to the owner/GM in active, archived and rollback views.

Shared multi-target damage waits until the final target and uses the successes in the complete shared damage roll for one added recoil obligation. Original chance effects and recharge still resolve; native Recoil attributes remain when Sheer Force trades a move's added effects, and the same retained-Recoil treatment applies to Life Orb. Matching King's Rock/Razor Fang chance effects now provide item-granted Sheer Force eligibility in the staged rules. Fixed set damage has no damage roll and receives neither extra damage dice nor rolled-success recoil; an already fainted user has no further recoil to resolve. Varied formulas now include reviewed type-booster dice as well as Life Orb without adding ordinary attributes, defense, STAB or critical bonuses to their literal formulas.

King's Rock adds one Flinch Chance Die to Special moves; Razor Fang adds one to Physical moves. Their matching-category added effects now make an otherwise eligible attack usable with Sheer Force's selected tradeoff. The two-die damage bonus is still awarded once when both the move and its item supply added effects. The staged varied-formula path also receives that dice bonus. Fixed set-damage moves have no dice pool to increase and retain their granted chance effects. Support variants such as Bide preparation and friendly/healing actions do not become damaging Sheer Force actions just because an item is equipped. Physical/Special matching and eligibility are checked at declaration, while the live item contribution is captured when damage begins and retained through the attack's remaining targets after loss. Item snapshots remain owner/GM-private across active, archived and rollback projections.

Chance resolution uses the ordinary owner/GM effect action and retains original move effects. A native Flinch pool merges with the item's die; Serene Grace and Wonder Skin adjust that merged pool once. Distinct paralysis/burn/etc. and item-Flinch pools remain separate. Flinch's once-per-round limit, type/ability immunity, Shield Dust, decoys, shields and holding back are respected. Teravolt/Turboblaze bypass the supported effect blockers; Mold Breaker's damage-only bypass does not erase Shield Dust. Magic Room/Klutz suppress the live contribution. Sonic Boom's typeless set damage can still carry its granted Flinch effect. Clash does not trigger these move-added effects, following Ruling page 71. Item evolution descriptions remain reference information and do not introduce an out-of-battle ability implementation.

Ball Fetch now has a GM-recorded failed-capture hook during an active battle. The command attests that a catch roll against a wild foe failed and that its action cost was already adjudicated; it does not simulate a catch roll, create a captured Pokemon or spend another Pokemon action. It validates a conscious deployed Ball Fetch user linked to the owning trainer, an untrained opposing Pokemon, a real owned catalog capture ball, and a stable nonduplicate attempt ID. Magic Room prevents a new throw. A held berry or other occupied Pokemon item slot does not prevent fetching.

A ball comes from the trainer's stock or a previously stored unit. Stock is materialized once into a tracked ball; a reused stored ball retains its identity, origin and usage. It stays protected until round end, then the same unit returns to the trainer's stored equipment. An earned return is not retroactively erased by a later ability change. Pending balls cannot simultaneously exist in equipment or another pending throw, and duplicate attempts/repeated round processing cannot mint balls. A throw cannot be recorded after that round's item returns, and pending returns must resolve before battle end.

Capture and round-end checkpoints include the affected trainer even when that trainer is not a combatant. Rollback restores stock, stored units and pending returns together. GM controls record the attested failure; the trainer owner and GM can see pending/returned balls, while other players' snapshots and inventory views are redacted. Automatic capture mechanics remain deferred. Ball Fetch's battle hook does not introduce Pickup/Honey Gather or other excluded out-of-battle ability effects.
